# EECS 494: Computer Game Design
EECS 494 is an upper-level elective typically chosen as an MDE-fulfilling course by a Senior in Computer Science. The course provides an introduction to the design and implementation of videogames, emphasizing rapid prototyping with an agile production methodology. EECS 494 is taught by [Jeremy Gibson](https://www.eecs.umich.edu/eecs/faculty/eecsfaculty.html?uniqname=gameprof) as of W15. The course textbook is [Introduction to Game Design, Prototyping, and Development](http://www.amazon.com/Introduction-Game-Design-Prototyping-Development/dp/0321933168) by Jeremy Gibson. The course website is [eecs.prototools.net/494](http://eecs.prototools.net/494/).

## Topics Encountered
EECS 494 attempts to provide students with two categories of knowledge. One, game design-- the ability to analyze and critique the ruleset of an interactive experience, and two, game implementation-- the ability to implement with a team a digital game to be played by an audience at a bi-annual showcase. The course also discusses the games industry, its nature, and how a student may "break in".

### Game Design
* Game Analysis Frameworks
* The Layered Tetrad
* Design Goals
* Paper Prototyping
* Playtesting
* The Mathematics of Game-Balance
* Puzzle Design
* Guiding the Player
* General experience design (The design of Disney Land)

### Game Implementation
* The Unity 3D Game Engine
* C# and MonoDevelop
* Component-based Software Architecture
* The Agile Methodology
* Project Scoping
* Git Version Control

### Game Industry
* Game Education
* Getting into the Industry
* Conferences (GDC, IndieCade)

## Expectations
EECS 494 has, in the past if not in the present, expected the following from students.
* Weekly Readings of a course Textbook.
* Implementations of tutorials from the course textbook.
* Recreation of a classic video game (NES era) using the Unity Game Engine.
* Rapid Prototype production (2 prototypes due in 2 weeks).
* Weekly presentation and exhibition of game projects in front of class.
* Maintenance, usage, and discussion of burndown charts (of the Agile methodology).
* Short papers (1-2 pages) serving as project postmortems and prior-art research.
* Optional partitipation in Wolverine Soft game jams (occasionally for extra credit).
* A multi-month project in teams of 4, to be demoed at an end-of-semester exhibition for the entire community.
* Produce trailers for projects.
* Record feedback for the projects of classmates. Receive feedback from classmates.
* Lead formal playtesting sessions of your and other team's projects.

## Should I take this?
EECS 494 has developed a reputation as a high-workload, high-reward course.
### Consider this course if...
* You like games.
* You want to work in the games industry.
* You want to work in interactive media or media-rich simulation.
* You work well with teams, or would like more practice in doing so.

### Be wary of this course if...
* You're ambivalent (or worse) about games.
* You have a busy schedule.
* Mildly subjective grading criteria scares you.
* You like to receive grades in a timely manner.

## Tips
### General
* Join [Wolverine Soft](http://www.wolverinesoft.org) and partake in their Unity tutorials.
* Spend a day walking through several example game projects in the course textbook.

### Team projects
* Establish consistent, weekly, in-person meeting times immediately.
* Be honest about the state of your project ("The controls are pretty unintuitive").
* Cast all criticism on the team, and not the individual. ("**We** messed up that presentation-- how can we improve?").
* Discuss individual responsibilities and progress. Talk honestly and openly about time-committment.
* Devote a chunk of time to testing pre-presentation.
