Before you register

Seat counts are a snapshot. The three-credit list was pulled from Atlas on 23 August 2026 and everything else on 24 August 2026. Open/waitlist status moves daily and Atlas itself only refreshes once per day — re-check anything you're counting on.

Prerequisites are not filtered. Several courses enforce them for real: ECON 431/461/483 want ECON 401; STRATEGY 350 wants STRATEGY 302; SI 526 wants SI 582 and 539; SI 649/670 want the SI programming sequence; CSE 593 wants graduate standing; ASTRO 206 wants PHYSICS 140; ARTDES 345/420/429/333 want studio prerequisites; ENTR 485 needs instructor consent. Expect to send override emails.

Variable credits. Atlas doesn't expose per-section credit for ENTR 390, ENTR 490, ENTR 599 (listed 1–15), EECS 498 and CSE 598 (1–4), PUBPOL 475 (1–4), or SI 364 (1–3). PHIL 424 is 2–3, PHIL 340 is 2–4, PHIL 560/578 and PHIL 611 are 1–3, and several PHIL and ECON courses are 3–4, where you elect the number. The credit tally uses the top of each range for the 4-credit courses and 3 for the open-ended ones. Ross mini-courses run half-term, so a 1.5-credit Ross course occupies its slot for roughly seven weeks, not fifteen — the week grid cannot show that.

Campus assignment. Every course is placed by its actual room against the Registrar's location-abbreviation list. Three are genuinely ambiguous and were called by hand: TO 448 + ARTDES 416 straddles both (Ross is Central, A&AB is North) and is marked North; SI 394 is listed in both 1250 USB and 1305 Leinweber and is marked Central; URP 489 and PHIL 611 have no room posted yet and are placed by department home. ENTR 560 differs by section — Tuesday is Central, Wednesday is North.

How complete is this? Fall 2026 has 8,190 course entries in Atlas. All of them were enumerated by title. How the evaluation numbers work. Atlas aggregates the last five academic years of official course evaluations across all instructors, so the scores describe the course, not necessarily the person teaching it this fall. Response rates vary a lot — each strip shows the raw count. Treat a 14-response score as a rumour and a 3,000-response score as a fact. Special-topics umbrellas (EECS 498, CSE 598, ENTR 390/490/599, PUBPOL 475) pool every section that ever ran under that number, so their scores say more about the department than about the specific section listed.
